# Press & community outreach

Fill in the bracketed bits. Keep it short — the single biggest reason pitches
get ignored is length. Nobody in a newsroom reads past the first paragraph of
a cold email.

**Decide first, together:** how much of your son's name, age and photo you're
comfortable putting out publicly. Some families are happy with a full name and
picture; others prefer "a local high school student." Both work for the story.
Settle it before you send anything, not after a producer asks.

---

## 1. Pitch email — radio stations and local media

Subject: **Local high schooler built an app for the DFW desi community**

> Hi [name],
>
> I'm [your name], from Little Elm. My son [name / "my son, a high school
> student here"] came up with an idea last year: one place for desi families
> to find local restaurants, deals, temple events, priests and caterers —
> instead of hunting across five WhatsApp groups.
>
> We built it together over the past few months. It went live in July as
> DesiZoom, and there are now [N] local businesses on it, mostly in Little Elm
> and Frisco. Restaurants take pickup orders directly instead of paying 30% to
> the delivery apps.
>
> I thought it might interest your listeners, especially heading into Diwali.
> Happy to come in, or to answer questions however suits you.
>
> www.desizoom.com
>
> Thanks for your time,
> [your name] · [phone]

**Why this works:** it leads with the story, not the product. A father-and-son
build is human interest; "new app launches" is a press release nobody runs.

## 2. Newsletter / temple bulletin blurb

Ready to print. Community newsletters are usually short of content and will
often use something like this verbatim.

> **A local app for our community**
>
> DesiZoom is a new app built right here in Little Elm by a father and son —
> the idea came from [name/"a local high school student"] who was tired of
> hunting through WhatsApp groups to find desi restaurants, deals and events.
>
> It brings together local desi restaurants offering pickup, community deals,
> temple and cultural events, and services like priests, caterers and mehndi
> artists — all in one place for the Little Elm, Frisco and Plano area.
>
> Local businesses can list for free. Temples and community organisations can
> post events at no cost.
>
> Have a look at www.desizoom.com

## 3. Who to contact

**Radio** — the four stations already in your own app, which is a nice opener
("we feature your stream in the app"):

- Radio Sangam 104.1 · FunAsia 94.5 · Big 106.2 · Beat 97.8

Ask for the community segment producer or the morning show, not "management."

**Temples and community organisations** — the ten already seeded in your
`organizations` table. Approach them with the free event ticketing offer *and*
the bulletin blurb together; you're giving them something before asking.

**Local press** — Community Impact (Little Elm / Frisco editions), Star Local
Media, Frisco Enterprise. Small local papers actively want stories like this.

**Indian-American outlets** — India Herald, local DFW desi community pages and
Facebook groups.

---

## 4. What to expect

Most won't reply. That's normal — send ten, expect one or two.

If a radio station bites it'll be a short live segment. Prepare three things:
what it is in one sentence, why your son thought of it, and what you'd say to a
restaurant owner listening. Nothing more; you'll have four minutes.

Best time to send: **now.** Diwali coverage gets planned three to four weeks
out, and you want to be in that planning, not chasing it afterwards.

## 5. One line for the site

Worth adding to the footer or an About page. It costs nothing and it makes
people root for you, which is exactly what a young marketplace needs.

> Built in Little Elm, Texas by a father and son.

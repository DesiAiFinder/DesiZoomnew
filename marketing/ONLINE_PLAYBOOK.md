# Online playbook — ready-to-post copy

Copy, paste, adjust the names. Everything here is written to sound like a
person in the community, because that's the only thing that works in desi
WhatsApp and Facebook groups.

**Sequencing rule:** don't post to a big group until the page looks full in
that city. A wide post to a thin marketplace burns the impression, and you
only get one per person. Warm channels first, merchant channels next, broad
channels last.

---

## 1. The highest-leverage post isn't yours

When a restaurant tells *their own* customers, it converts several times
better than anything you post. Trust is already there. Give every merchant
this the day they go live — send it to them written out, so they only have to
forward it.

**For the merchant to send to their WhatsApp list / status:**

> 🎉 You can now order from us directly on DesiZoom — no delivery app fees,
> no marked-up prices.
>
> Same menu, same kitchen, straight from us. Order ahead and pick up when it's
> ready.
>
> 👉 [their deep link]
>
> Thanks for supporting us 🙏

**For their Instagram story:** same words, shorter, with the QR from the
counter card as the image and a link sticker.

Ask for this explicitly at signup. "One thing that would really help — send
this to your WhatsApp list once." Most say yes; almost none think of it
themselves.

---

## 2. Your own warm channels (start here today)

Your personal groups, your building, your temple circle. Low risk, forgiving
audience, and honest about being early.

> Hi all — some of you know I've been building something for our community
> here. It's called DesiZoom — desi restaurants, deals, events and services
> for Little Elm / Frisco / Plano, in one place.
>
> It's early and I'm adding businesses every week. If you order pickup from a
> desi place, have a look — and tell me what's missing, that's genuinely more
> useful to me right now than anything else.
>
> www.desizoom.com

Being visibly early is an advantage in warm channels. People help things that
are being built. They don't help things that are being sold.

---

## 3. Community Facebook / WhatsApp groups

One post per group per week, maximum. Post as a member. If it reads like an
ad, you get removed and you don't get a second chance.

**Deal-led (best performing):**

> Anyone else starting Diwali shopping? Couple of Little Elm places doing
> pickup deals right now — Desi Bandhu has free chai with any order, and
> there's a sweets box deal at [X]. Both on DesiZoom if useful 🪔

**Question-led (highest engagement, no link):**

> Where's everyone getting Diwali sweets this year? Trying to find something
> in Little Elm / Frisco that isn't a 40-minute drive.

Then reply in the comments with what you found, mentioning the app naturally.
Engagement-first posts outperform link-first posts by a wide margin, and
groups don't penalise them.

**Never post:**

> 🎉 DOWNLOAD DESIZOOM! Best deals in DFW! Link in comments! 🎉

The test: would you post it if you didn't own the app?

## 4. Recruiting businesses online

Different audience, different tone. Desi business-owner groups, Chamber of
Commerce pages, restaurant owner circles.

> Restaurant owners — if you're taking pickup orders through the delivery
> apps, you're paying a big cut and they keep your customer list.
>
> I built DesiZoom for desi businesses in DFW. 6% on orders, no monthly fee,
> no tablet, no contract. Orders come to your phone. Money goes straight to
> your bank.
>
> And I'll set up your menu for you — just send me a photo of it.
>
> Happy to show anyone how it works, no pressure: [your number]

Lead with their pain, not your product. "They keep your customer list" lands
harder than any percentage.

---

## 5. Nextdoor and local subreddits

Both are hostile to promotion and useful when you're genuinely local. Only
post if you can be helpful first.

Nextdoor works best as a recommendation:
> For anyone looking for Indian food nearby — Desi Bandhu in Little Elm does
> pickup, and their samosas are excellent. They're on DesiZoom for ordering
> ahead.

Reddit (r/Dallas, r/FriscoTX): only participate in existing threads asking
where to find Indian groceries or restaurants. Never make a promotional post.
Reddit will find out you own it, and it will go badly.

---

## 6. Google Business Profile

Free, and it's how people actually find local things. Claim a profile for
DesiZoom, and separately make sure every merchant you sign has claimed theirs.
Half of small desi restaurants haven't — offering to fix that for them is a
genuine favour and a great reason to walk back in.

---

## 7. Diwali campaign (the one dated moment)

You have roughly seven weeks. Diwali is the single highest-intent shopping
period of the desi year, and it's the one time people actively search for
sweets, catering, pooja items and events.

**Two weeks before:**
> Diwali is [N] days away. Sweets, catering, pooja samagri and priests — all
> the Little Elm / Frisco places in one page: www.desizoom.com/deals
> Order early, they sell out 🪔

**Merchant angle, four weeks before:**
> Diwali orders start coming in about three weeks out. If you want to take
> pre-orders for sweets or catering, I can have you set up this week.

**During:** post photos of real orders. Nothing sells a marketplace like
evidence it's being used.

---

## What actually moves the needle, ranked

1. A merchant telling their own customers
2. Someone posting a real deal they found, unprompted
3. Your own warm-channel posts
4. Community group posts
5. Everything else

Notice that the top two aren't things you do — they're things you *enable*.
Which is why supply comes first and broadcasting comes second.

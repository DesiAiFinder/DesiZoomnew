# Field kit — what to actually say

Read this on your phone before you walk in. Everything here is meant to be
said out loud, not read off a page.

---

## Before you go

- Go **2:30–5pm**. Lunch rush is over, dinner hasn't started. Anyone who walks
  into a desi restaurant at 7pm asking for the owner gets nothing.
- Ask for the **owner or manager by name** if you can see it on Google. "Is
  Ramesh in?" opens doors that "can I speak to the manager?" does not.
- Bring: printed one-pager, your phone with the app already open, and a
  charged battery. The phone demo is the whole pitch.
- Target 5 per evening. Expect 1 to say yes. That's a normal, good day.

---

## The 40-second pitch

> "Hi — I'm Laxman, I run DesiZoom. It's a local app for desi families in
> Little Elm and Frisco — food, deals, events, all in one place.
>
> Quick question: are you on DoorDash?
>
> [If yes] Right, so they take a big cut. We take 6%, and there's no monthly
> fee and no tablet. But honestly the bigger thing is you keep your customer —
> their name, their number, their repeat orders. On DoorDash that's theirs.
>
> Here — [open phone] this is what an order looks like on your side. It rings
> like a text, you tap once to accept, tap again when it's ready. That's it.
>
> Diwali's about seven weeks out. If you want to be listed before that rush,
> send me a photo of your menu on WhatsApp tonight and you'll be live tomorrow.
> I do the typing. Costs you nothing to try."

Then **stop talking**. Let them ask something.

---

## The demo is the close

Do not skip this. Have the app open on your phone before you walk in.

Show them, in this order:
1. The Deals page in their city — real listings, real businesses.
2. A restaurant menu with the ordering flow.
3. **The live order bar.** Place a test order from a second phone if you can,
   so they watch the alert land and hear the chime. Nothing you say beats this.

Owners don't buy a commission rate. They buy "oh, that's simple."

---

## Objections you will actually hear

**"DoorDash only charges me 6% for pickup too."**
True — and don't pretend otherwise, they'll know. Say:
> "You're right, pickup is 6% there too. Two differences. One, if you ever do
> delivery, they're 15 to 30 and we're still 6. Two — on DoorDash most people
> mark their menu up 15–20% to survive the commission, so your regulars pay
> more. Here you don't have to. And their customer list is theirs. Mine is
> yours."

**"I don't have time to set this up."**
> "You don't have to. Take a photo of your menu right now, WhatsApp it to me,
> and I'll have it live tomorrow. If it's wrong you tell me and I fix it."

**"Nobody uses your app yet."**
Be honest. Defensiveness kills it.
> "Correct, we're new — launched a few weeks ago in Little Elm. That's exactly
> why it's free to list and free to post deals. You're not paying for an
> audience; you're getting in before there's a queue. And you lose nothing —
> no monthly fee, no contract. If it does nothing for you, you've lost a photo
> of your menu."

**"I already have my own website / ordering."**
> "Good — keep it. This isn't a replacement, it's discovery. People who don't
> know you exist yet. When they find you here they can order, and next time
> they'll come to you directly. That's fine by me."

**"How and when do I get paid?"**
> "Straight to your bank through Stripe, same as any card payment — usually two
> business days. It doesn't sit with me."

**"What's the catch?"**
> "6% on orders. That's the whole business model. Deals and your listing are
> free."

## The WhatsApp follow-up (send within 2 hours)

Send it the same evening while they still remember your face.

> Hi [Name] — Laxman from DesiZoom, we spoke at [restaurant] this afternoon.
>
> Whenever you're ready just send a photo of your menu here and I'll set
> everything up — usually live the next day. No charge to list.
>
> Here's what your page will look like: [link to a live restaurant]
>
> No rush, and thanks for your time today.

Do not send more than one follow-up. If they don't reply, walk back in two
weeks with something new to say ("we're up to nine restaurants now").

## Community group posts

Post as a member, not a business. One post per group per week, maximum.
Anything that reads like an ad gets you removed, and you don't get a second
chance in a group.

**Good:**
> Anyone else ordering Diwali sweets early this year? Found a few Little Elm
> places doing pickup on DesiZoom — Desi Bandhu has a box deal on right now.
> Sharing in case it's useful 🪔

**Bad:**
> 🎉 DOWNLOAD DESIZOOM TODAY! Best deals in DFW! Link below! 🎉

The difference is whether you'd post it if you didn't own the app.

## Track it, or you're guessing

One row per business. A spreadsheet is fine.

| Business | Type | Contact | Visited | Outcome | Follow-up | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Outcomes worth recording: `signed` · `menu pending` · `interested, revisit` ·
`no` · `owner not in`.

"Owner not in" is the most common first result and is not a rejection — it
means go back Tuesday.

## Week one checklist

- [ ] Print 20 one-pagers
- [ ] Build the prospect list from your own Deals page (Google Places block,
      sorted by rating — that's a ready-made list of every desi restaurant near
      you)
- [ ] Two evenings out, 5 businesses each
- [ ] Follow up every conversation the same night on WhatsApp
- [ ] Saturday: temple + grocery circuit, ask about their Diwali event
- [ ] Sunday: post the week's new deals in 3 community groups
- [ ] Count: conversations had, menus received, businesses live

Ten conversations a week for six weeks gets you to Diwali with roughly fifteen
merchants. That's the whole plan.

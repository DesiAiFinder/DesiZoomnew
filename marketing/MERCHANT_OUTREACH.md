# Merchant outreach — first 30

Pulled from Google Places on 22 October 2026: every desi restaurant, grocery,
sweet shop and caterer within 15 miles of Little Elm. 82 found; the 30 in
`merchant-targets.csv` are the ones within 8.3 miles, sorted by review count.

Review count is the ranking signal, not rating. A place with 2,000 reviews has
real order volume, which means a real delivery-app bill — and that bill is the
whole conversation.

---

## Priority

**A (14)** — 150+ reviews, within 8 miles. Start here. Enough volume that 6%
versus 30% is material to them.

**B (13)** — solid but either smaller or further out. Second pass.

**C (3)** — the two caterers and one small grocery. Low review counts, but
caterers are worth a separate conversation: a single $1,000 catering order is
worth more to you than fifty biryanis, and they have no DoorDash option at all.

---

## Go in person

Not email. Not a phone call. Walk in **between 2:30 and 4:30pm**, when the
lunch rush is done and dinner prep hasn't started. Ask for the owner by
saying you want to talk about online orders. If they're busy, leave the flyer
and come back — don't pitch a manager who can't decide.

Order something first. You're a customer before you're a salesperson.

---

## What to say

Keep it under thirty seconds before you stop talking.

> "Hi — I'm Laxman, I live in Little Elm. My son and I built a site for desi
> restaurants to take pickup orders directly. We charge 6%. DoorDash takes
> about 30% on delivery and around 15% on pickup. There's no monthly fee, no
> tablet, no contract — if it doesn't work for you, you take your listing
> down. Can I show you on my phone?"

Then show them. Not a deck — the actual site, their own category, how an order
arrives, what the kitchen sees.

**Have ready:** your phone with desizoom.com open, printed flyers, and a
willingness to set the whole thing up for them on the spot. The setup *is*
the close — an owner who says "send me a link" never does it.

---

## The questions you will get

**"How many customers do you have?"**
Be honest. "We just went live. You'd be one of the first, which is why I'm
offering to set it all up myself and why there's no fee of any kind." Then
turn it around: "the first few restaurants are the ones people find, because
there's nothing else on there yet."

Never invent a number. One owner checking and finding it untrue ends you in a
community this size.

**"How do I get paid?"**
Straight to your own bank via Stripe. A few business days once you're running;
the first payout can take up to two weeks while Stripe verifies the account.

**"What if I want to stop?"**
Take the listing down. No notice, no fee, nothing to cancel.

**"Who handles refunds?"**
We do, through the platform, and we talk to you first. Commission comes back
to you on a refunded order — you're not charged on money you didn't keep.

**"Is this like DoorDash?"**
No. Pickup only, the customer comes to you, and you keep the customer. We're
not a delivery company and we don't rent you demand.

---

## The catering angle

The two caterers are a different pitch and probably a better one. Catering
orders are large, there's no delivery app serving them, and discovery today is
entirely word of mouth. For them the pitch isn't "cheaper than DoorDash", it's
"people searching for a caterer in Frisco find nothing — be the listing they
find."

---

## What to track

A row per visit: who you spoke to, whether they're on DoorDash, what they said
no to. Three "we already have DoorDash" answers means the pitch needs to lead
with something else. The objections are the useful output of the first week,
more than the signups.

---

## Honest expectation

Thirty visits, maybe three or four sign up. That is a *good* result for a cold
first week with no track record. The ones who say no aren't gone — they're
waiting to see whether the first few get orders.

Which is why the real work after signing a restaurant is getting customers to
it. A merchant who signs and sees nothing for a month tells everyone.
